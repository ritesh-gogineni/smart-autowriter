# Writing style (applies to every article)

## Facts
- Know today's date. Never describe a past event as upcoming, or the reverse.
- Verify every date, time (in the site's timezone), figure and ranking against an official source. If an official source disagrees with the brief, the official source wins; say so in notes.md.
- Forecasts, predictions and unconfirmed reports are labelled as such in the sentence.
- Write in your own words. Use short direct quotes only, exactly as spoken or written.
- Get names, roles, ages and affiliations right. Take extra care with minors: neutral and kind.

## Citations: only where needed (over-citing reads badly)
CITE (name the outlet or person in the sentence, linked on first mention with `target="_blank" rel="noopener"`):
- rumours, reports and any claim that is not established fact (a rumour stays 'a report' until confirmed);
- quotes (who said it and when);
- news that comes from a media report rather than the primary source;
- specific statistics that are not obvious (records, model outputs, 'most since 1998'), with the named source.

DO NOT CITE: what plainly happened (results, who took part), schedules, widely known facts.
A typical article has 2-5 source links. Never cite aggregators, betting or tips sites, or fan blogs. Add 2-4 natural internal links to the related posts listed in the brief.

## Do not sound like AI
1. No quotable sign-offs ending sections. End on the last useful fact: the time, the next event, the prediction.
2. No will-tell-us endings ('Saturday will tell us plenty', '...will show whether...').
3. No signposting before the point ('The numbers explain the dilemma.', 'Two details matter.', 'One detail worth noting is'). Just make the point.
4. Use contractions (isn't, doesn't, it's). Leave quotes exactly as spoken.
5. Balanced pairs and colons: at most one per article. First / Second / Third: rarely.
6. No bracketed source tags and no source footers.
7. No false precision ('We rate the fit 8.6 out of 10').
8. Vary the skeleton: no 'In this article' box, no fixed five-H2 template, no 'Our view' closer every time. Subheads only when the piece is long enough.
9. No em-dashes outside quotes. No rhetorical one-word questions ('The catch?'). No single-word dramatic sentences. No 'isn't just X, it's Y'. No 'Here's the thing', 'Make no mistake', 'Only time will tell', 'It remains to be seen', 'Buckle up'.
- Banned words in the body: explosive, bombshell, seismic, stunning, shocking, jaw-dropping, staggering, brutal, masterclass, audacious, mouth-watering, nightmare, chaos, heist, saga (max once), genuinely, precisely, crucially, perfect storm, game-changer, delve, tapestry, testament, landscape, pivotal, unwavering, seamless, meticulous, revolution, narrative, intriguing subplot, uncharted territory, remarkable, notable shift, first and foremost. The niche pack may add more.
- What reads human: an opinion with a name on it, short sentences next to long ones, one concrete detail, stopping when the information runs out. Where it fits naturally, take a clear stance some readers will disagree with, because comments thrive on debate.
- Use the spelling variant of the site's language setting. Open with the news in the first sentence or two, paying off the headline's hook. End plainly with what happens next.
- Before finishing, search your draft for em-dashes, 'is not', 'will tell', 'matters', 'worth noting' and the banned words, and fix every hit. Re-check every date, time and name.

## Format
- Clean HTML only: p, h2, h3, ul, li, strong, a, table, div and span with inline styles. No markdown, no scripts, no iframes, no external images or fonts inside the content.
- Length follows the brief; do not pad.
- The brief fixes the headline set. Do not change it; make the opening paragraphs pay off its hook.
